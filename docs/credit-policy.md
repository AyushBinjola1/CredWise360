# Simulated credit policy

The C++ engine chooses a strategy using the active version of the PostgreSQL scoring policy. Conventional assessment requires both the minimum observed repayment count and the minimum history length. A thin file takes the alternative route only with borrower consent. If fewer than the required number of weighted observations are available, the result carries a null score and recommends human review.

Each available factor has a value between 0 and 100. Its effective weight is its configured weight divided by the sum of the available positive weights. The simulated score is 300 plus six times the weighted mean factor value, rounded to the nearest integer. Conventional factors cover on-time repayment share, unused credit limit, outstanding debt relative to annual income, history duration, recent distinct applications, income regularity and emergency coverage. The policy supplies each reference threshold. The score has no connection to a credit bureau.

With consent, the alternative strategy can use on-time rent, on-time utility bills, income regularity, savings relative to the configured savings rate and the remaining share of the monthly EMI limit. A blank optional percentage is null and carries no weight. A reported 0% carries its full eligible weight. Monthly savings and existing EMI carry separate availability flags so a budget default cannot become alternative evidence. Existing zero values stored before the availability migration are treated as unknown. A borrower can report zero explicitly or clear an observation.

Additional monthly EMI capacity is the larger of zero and the smaller of two amounts. The first is the policy share of monthly income less existing EMI. The second is the policy share of monthly disposable income. Capacity uses integer paise; the product quote must validate its own EMI against that headroom. A customer with sufficient evidence can still have zero repayment capacity.

The current full assessment updates inside the transaction that edits the financial profile. The score history adds a row only when the score changes, including a transition to or from insufficient evidence. Policy edits produce immutable revisions with the administrator's reason. A consistency check runs equivalent financial profiles with different synthetic demographic labels through that same policy and compares the outputs.
